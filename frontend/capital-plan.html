§1 · The rules of the money

Six principles that fix the order.

The sequence in §4 is not a preference — it is forced by these rules. State them up front so the order reads as discipline, not opinion.

Principle 0 · written policy
Restricted capital is never deployed to speculative yield.
Grant funds are contractually earmarked for the project. Diverting them to a liquidity pool or farm to "earn while idle" is misuse of restricted funds — and the yields advertised there (0.5–1%/day) are not real cash flow, they fail the same frozen-deposit litmus we print on the vault. Policy: idle project treasury sits in capital-preservation instruments only (regulated stable lending / tokenized T-bills at single-digit annual yield), disclosed, and never as personal investment. This is the failure mode we refuse, in writing.
Principle 1
Non-dilutive capital first, in a stack.
Take grant + ecosystem money before any equity. The grant is a catalyst and a credential, not the whole budget — so we co-fund each big rock from its natural source (the audit from the audit grant, runway from the initial, the hire from the KPI bonus).
Principle 2
Hire against proof, not hope.
The first engineering hire is triggered by a KPI tranche landing — i.e. the ecosystem pays for the headcount once we've proven traction — not by the initial grant, which is too small to carry a salary and a build at once.
Principle 3
The audit is funded by the audit grant, not operating runway.
A real audit ($20–80k+) would consume the entire initial grant. So the initial grant carries only a co-pay / contest-bounty seed; the bulk comes from the Optimism Audit Grant (GIWA is OP Stack, so we qualify). Operating runway stays runway.
Principle 4
Legal / RWA review is ring-fenced and non-negotiable.
The headline mainnet yield strategy is a KRW-treasury RWA product. It touches securities law across jurisdictions. We do not ship it without a signed legal review — so that cost is reserved on day one, not discovered later.
Principle 5
Tranches are gated by KPIs we can actually hit.
We set milestones to our real confidence, never over-promised. A missed KPI tranche is the #1 first-timer failure; an achievable-then-beaten KPI is the credibility compounding machine.

§6 · How the suite earns (the model)

Revenue, by driver and by cohort.

Three different engines — a B2B SaaS line (high margin, near-term, fully in our control), an AUM engine (the vault), and a volume engine (the DEX, thin early, a moat more than a paycheck). Plus additional methods in §7. All figures are the locked canonical model.

Per 1,000 onboarded users · year one

The cohort math, shown

  • WonVault: 25% deposit × $300 avg = $75k AUM → entry fee (1.0% blended) ≈ $750 one-time + management (1%/yr) ≈ $62/mo + performance (15% of 5% yield) ≈ $47/mo.
  • GIWASwap: 20% swap × $400/mo volume = $80k/mo → protocol fee (0.08%) ≈ $64/mo.

  • Cohort total ≈ ~$1.8–2k of value in year one, of which ~$1k/yr is recurring while the cohort stays. Apply ~50% time-weighted 12-month retention.

§7 · Additional income methods

Beyond the three core fees.

Ordered by how soon each is real. The first three are near-term and sales-led — i.e. in our control.

  • White-label / embedded-wallet licensing — license the trust-tiered vault + swap widgets to other apps/wallets for a SaaS fee + rev-share on the flow. The Naver-Pay thesis as a product line. Near-term, high margin.
  • Treasury-as-a-service — park a DAO/protocol's stablecoins in the RWA vault; earn management + performance on institutional AUM. One $5M treasury dwarfs thousands of retail users. Near-term, sales-led.
  • Compliance / credit rail (B2B) — DojangScore as a KYC/Sybil/credit gate other protocols integrate (the premium API scaled into a compliance product). Near-term.
  • Curator fees (MetaMorpho-style) — run curated, isolated vaults for institutions; take a curator cut. Mid-term.
  • FX / on-off-ramp rev-share — when the KRW stablecoin flows through the vault/swap, take a slice of the ramp provider's fee via partnership. Mid-term, partnership-led.
  • Anonymized aggregate data — trust-tier distribution / flow analytics to market-makers / exchanges / compliance firms; privacy-respecting only. Later.
  • Retro Funding + future ecosystem grants — non-dilutive cash that funds the company without dilution; budgeted as a line, not a hope. Ongoing.
What we deliberately skip: NFT membership tiers, pay-to-list on the DEX, and any rent-seeking that erodes the trust brand. The edge is being the honest, regulated, institutional-grade yield layer; monetization that smells like extraction undercuts the thesis.
§8 · Operator vs. protocol (written down)

Who keeps which fee.

Today the deployer controls the treasury/owner address, so the split is a design decision. We make it in the open, because a partner who shares the upside gets more distribution than one who pockets everything.

Fee Policy Why
Performance fee (vault) Operator carry Earned only when users earn — perfectly aligned; the uncapped upside for building it.
Entry + management fee (vault) Shared with ecosystem (e.g. 50/50) Gives GIWA a recurring stake in our success → they push distribution at us.
On-chain query fee (DojangScore) Split operator / protocol Volume-dependent; the protocol benefits from a trusted credit rail.
Premium API / white-label / TaaS Operator Off-chain services we build and host; rev-share where a partner's flow is involved.
Swap protocol fee Expect a protocol/chain cut On many chains this is the chain's fee; we plan for it, not against it.
§9 · Risk register & contingencies

What if a tranche doesn't land.

A plan with no plan B is a hope. Each risk below has a named fallback.

Risk Impact Contingency
Initial grant smaller than ~$20k Runway shrinks Cut stipend months first (operator absorbs); keep legal ring-fence + API-build intact (they create the escape velocity). Founder's own capital covers the infra floor.
KPI tranche slips / missed Hire delayed Stay lean; the hire was gated on proof anyway (Principle 2). Re-scope the KPI to the achievable-then-beat band; lean on B2B revenue to hold the line.
Optimism Audit Grant denied Audit unfunded Fallback ladder: a competitive contest (Code4rena/Sherlock) → a smaller/cheaper auditor for v1 → a phased audit (core vault first, periphery later). Never ship real-money unaudited.
GIWA mainnet / KRW stablecoin delayed Consumer funnel late The crypto-native base + B2B SaaS do not depend on it; model the consumer funnel as upside, not base case. Ship the RWA strategy to whatever regulated stable asset is available first.
Legal review blocks the RWA strategy Headline yield late Launch WonVault on a non-securities lending/fee strategy first; the RWA vault follows legal clearance. The ring-fence means the cost is already paid either way.
§10 · The capital-efficiency loop

Non-dilutive in, milestones out, repeat.

$ of non-dilutive capital → months of lean runway → shipped, audited milestones → the next non-dilutive source (KPI bonus / audit grant / retro funding / B2B revenue) → repeat — until the product's own fees self-fund the company. Equity is touched last, from strength, or not at all.the whole plan in one sentence

The $20,000 is not the budget. It is the first move in a sequence of non-dilutive moves that buys time to build credibility and metrics without giving up the company — and it is spent, line by line, in the order the dependencies force. That is the answer to "do you know what our money buys and in what order." Yes: every dollar, every month, every gate — and the written policy that keeps us from ever gambling it.
